\begin{abstract}
\(p\)-Adic numbers replace magnitude-based closeness by agreement of successive
radix digits. We derive this construction, explain negative and fractional
values, and distinguish an infinite field, finite residue arithmetic and
hierarchical codes. These distinctions motivate hybrid language-model
components without implying that residues approximate real neural states.
Verified arithmetic and pretrained-projection controls show exact equivalence
of binary residues to wrapping integers; narrow reconstruction damages
real-valued likelihood. We then train three output-supervised Q/K bridges in
frozen SmolLM2-360M and evaluate binary/ternary routing with 128 shared selected
positions per query-head group. An untouched 40-article WikiText screen uses
20,000 paired document resamples and a prespecified 1\% PPL allowance.
Both families pass all required seed/comparator/endpoint bounds; their
largest stock-reference affected-target upper PPL changes are 0.774\% and
0.523\%. A separately frozen native single-query evaluation on 40 PG-19
books at 2,048/4,096 tokens fails the joint screen: binary passes 23/36
required bounds and ternary 29/36, with every full-reference bound failing
at 4,096. Complete numerical and independent statistical audits verify
these decisions. Ordinary coarsened binary and coverage controls have
smaller point losses. Secondary component-cost characterization passes 78
correctness cases and an independent audit of 378,000 samples on Spark;
the original interrupted allocation is preserved separately.
A binary finite-ring/ball FFN pilot fits a real teacher-supervised readout,
but its fixed-readout digit search accepts no ring changes in three seeds;
complete FFN replacement strongly damages development likelihood.
A separate CLINC150 probe learns meaningful semantic prefixes and outperforms
shuffled prefixes on seen intents, but stronger flat controls and failed
unseen-intent progression prevent a quality-advantage claim.
A continuous-disk answer branch learns exact modular rules; fixed fusion with
LLM priors improves on stock but fails stronger real Fourier controls.
First-layer intervention, fixed fits, retained real KV and the combined
operator/corpus/context change limit interpretation. The results motivate
explicit encoding, resolution and locality controls and establish a scoped
negative transfer result. They support limited feasibility of a learned
finite-hierarchy component, rather than broad \(p\)-adic utility or a universal
advantage of one prime. Implementation latency is not the criterion for
accepting or rejecting the mathematical concept.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Language-model efficiency is often approached through small numerical alphabets.
Native ternary weights, exemplified by BitNet b1.58, and hardware-oriented
floating-point recipes such as NVFP4 reduce different parts of storage and
computation \citep{ma2024era,ma2025bitnet,nvidia2026nvfp4}. Their benefits depend
on scaling metadata, accumulator precision, training and implementation.
\(p\)-Adic numbers ask a different question: what changes if closeness means
agreement under successive divisibility tests rather than small real error?

This question has two component-level motivations. Finite residues support
exact arithmetic at nested resolutions, while balls in a \(p\)-adic metric
provide explicit hierarchies for addresses and candidate sets. A language
model could use such a hierarchy to retrieve keys, share features or represent
structured objects while retaining real-valued attention and likelihood.
The connection must be learned or designed: the divisibility of two numerical
codes does not by itself establish semantic similarity.

Our primary scientific question is \emph{whether finite \(p\)-adic computation
or geometry can form a useful, learnable component of an LLM}. The conceptual
case concerns model quality, representation and an interpretable mechanism
against matched ordinary controls. A localized benefit would suffice;
superiority on every metric is unnecessary. Execution speed on current
hardware is a secondary implementation question. A whole pretrained Transformer
cannot be justified by replacing each float with a residue, because the
arithmetic, topology and readout would all change.

The main text develops the mathematical construction and real interfaces,
then reports the central quality outcomes. The SI contains every original
numerical table, development control, failed gate, statistical procedure and
reproducibility record. The evidence supports limited first-layer quality
non-inferiority on WikiText and a failed transfer screen for the distinct
native PG-19 operator. This supports a scoped feasibility statement, while
transfer and attribution remain unresolved. Secondary resource measurements
describe the current implementation and establish no hardware advantage.

\section{Related work and scope}

\(p\)-Adic neural constructions precede current LLMs
\citep{albeverio1999networks,baker2022pluralisation,martins2025learning}.
Character activations, ball-indicator learning and non-Archimedean tree
architectures provide further mathematical precedents
\citep{mihara2026character,nguessan2025vpunn,zuniga2026deep}.
A recent hull-based approach makes disk radii continuous while retaining exact
\(p\)-adic centers, enabling directional optimization
\citep{salazar2026continuous}. Its released code is still being refactored;
our independent small learning check and modular answer interface are scoped
separately in SI Section~\ref{si:continuous-learning}.
Finite precision and its propagation are established numerical mathematics
\citep{caruso2017computations}. Our arithmetic identities and exact tree
implementations are controls, rather than new theorems.

Learned or geometric sparsity also has strong precedents. MoBA, NSA and HiP
use block selection or hierarchical pruning \citep{lu2025moba,yuan2025nsa,lee2025hip};
Quest, SnapKV and KIVI address KV selection or compression
\citep{tang2024quest,li2024snapkv,liu2024kivi}. ZETA learns binary Z-order
addresses, and MiniMax Sparse Attention and Hierarchical Global Attention
further constrain claims about learned group-aware routing
\citep{zeng2025zeta,lai2026msa,hga2026routing}.
Their complete systems and reported performance are not reproduced here.

We therefore claim neither the first \(p\)-adic attention mechanism nor novelty
of learned radix addresses, tree evaluation or selected real-KV readout.
The relevant question concerns the actual encoding, resolution, training
and physical-access tradeoff against matched controls. Detailed literature
boundaries and pinned public-code inspections are in SI
Sections~\ref{si:related} and~\ref{app:public-code}.

\section{From positional digits to \texorpdfstring{\(p\)}{p}-adic numbers}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{A radix alone does not define a number system}

Every nonnegative integer can be written in base \(p\):
\[
 n=a_0+a_1p+\cdots+a_rp^r,\qquad a_j\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}.
\]
For example, \(31=1+2+4+8+16=(11111)_2\), while
\(31=1+1\cdot3+0\cdot9+1\cdot27=(1011)_3\).
In both bases this is the same ordinary integer.
Changing the radix alone does not change its arithmetic or notion of error.
The \(p\)-adic construction instead changes which digit agreements count as
close and consequently which infinite series converge.

Write digits in the usual order, with the coefficient of \(p^0\) at the
right. Real positional approximation ordinarily refines the small fractional
powers toward the right of the radix point. A \(p\)-adic approximation first
fixes the units digit, then the \(p\) digit, then the \(p^2\) digit, proceeding
toward the left. High powers of \(p\) will be small in the new metric. Thus
``low-power'' describes the exponent, not a small real contribution.

\subsection{Deriving closeness from divisibility}

For a nonzero rational, remove all factors of a fixed prime \(p\):
\[
 x=p^e\frac{a}{b},\qquad e\in\Z,\quad p\nmid a,\quad p\nmid b.
\]
Define its valuation \(\vp(x)=e\) and norm \(|x|_p=p^{-e}\), with
\(\vp(0)=+\infty\) and \(|0|_p=0\). The distance is
\(d_p(x,y)=|x-y|_p\). For integers, matching \(\ell\) low-power digits means
\[
 x\equiv y\pmod{p^\ell}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \vp(x-y)\geq\ell\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_p(x,y)\leq p^{-\ell}.
\]
This follows because the matched part cancels and the remaining difference
is a multiple of \(p^\ell\). If the next digits differ, the distance is exactly
\(p^{-\ell}\). For example, 1 and 17 match four binary low-power digits;
their real separation is 16, but their 2-adic distance is \(1/16\).

The prime assumption matters: it gives
\(\vp(xy)=\vp(x)+\vp(y)\), hence \(|xy|_p=|x|_p|y|_p\).
A naive count of factors of a composite base need not satisfy this property;
for base 6, neither 2 nor 3 has a factor of 6, while their product does.
Composite-base inverse-limit rings can be constructed, but they are a
different object and need not be fields. Bases 2, 3 and 5 are therefore
natural comparisons here; an arbitrary radix is not interchangeable with a
prime in this field construction.

Common divisibility survives addition:
\(\vp(x+y)\geq\min(\vp(x),\vp(y))\). Consequently,
\[
 |x+y|_p\leq\max(|x|_p,|y|_p),\qquad
 d_p(x,z)\leq\max(d_p(x,y),d_p(y,z)).
\]
This strong triangle inequality makes the metric \emph{ultrametric}.
Cancellation may make a sum still closer to zero; it cannot violate the
bound. Balls that intersect are nested when their radii are ordered, so
neighborhoods have a tree structure rather than real interpolation geometry.

\subsection{Completion and compatible finite approximations}

The rational sequence \(p^N\) tends to zero in this metric, since
\(|p^N|_p=p^{-N}\). Therefore partial sums
\(s_K=\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}a_jp^j\) are Cauchy: for \(L>K\),
\(s_L-s_K\) is divisible by \(p^K\). Their missing limits are supplied by
completing \(\Q\) under \(d_p\), just as completing it under real absolute
value supplies \(\R\). The result is the field \(\Qp\), containing series
\[
 x=\sum_{j=e}^{\infty}a_jp^j,\qquad e\in\Z.
\]
The nonzero leading digit gives \(\vp(x)=e\). Each value has only finitely
many negative-power digits, but may have infinitely many nonnegative-power
digits. The integral subring is \(\Zp=\{x:\vp(x)\geq0\}\); its expansions
start at exponent zero. These are alternative completions of the rational
numbers, rather than additional real decimal places.

There is an equivalent computational construction. Record residues
\(r_K\in\Z/p^K\Z\) at every precision with
\[
 r_{K+1}\bmod p^K=r_K,\qquad
 \Zp\cong\varprojlim_K\Z/p^K\Z.
\]
Compatibility makes each newly resolved digit extend the existing ones.
Addition and multiplication are performed at every precision and commute
with reduction. Allowing powers of \(p\) in the denominator gives
\(\Qp=\Zp[1/p]\). These digit, metric and inverse-limit views describe the
same objects \citep{caruso2017computations}.

\subsection{Negative integers without a sign bit}

Negative ordinary integers already belong to \(\Zp\). Their finite residues
are compatible: \(-1\bmod p^K=p^K-1\). Expanding these residues gives
\[
 -1=(p-1)+(p-1)p+(p-1)p^2+\cdots.
\]
Indeed, the sum through exponent \(K-1\) is \(p^K-1\), whose difference
from \(-1\) has norm \(p^{-K}\to0\). Thus
\(-1=(\ldots111)_2=(\ldots222)_3\), whereas
\(-2=2+2^2+2^3+\cdots=(\ldots1110)_2\).
Adding 1 to the first expansion produces a carry through all resolved digits
and yields zero at every finite precision.

There is no natural field order corresponding to the familiar real number
line. The label ``negative'' here identifies an embedded negative rational;
a general \(p\)-adic value does not carry an intrinsic real sign. A finite
machine implementation may choose a balanced signed decoder for real
readout. That choice is separate from the \(p\)-adic digits themselves.
For \(p=2\), balanced residue decoding coincides exactly with ordinary
fixed-width two's-complement wrapping arithmetic (SI Section~\ref{si:arithmetic}).

\subsection{Fractions, unit inverses and the radix point}

For a reduced rational \(a/b\),
\(\vp(a/b)=\vp(a)-\vp(b)\). It is integral in \(\Zp\) precisely when
\(p\nmid b\); thus ``\(p\)-adic integer'' does not mean ordinary integer.
If \(p\nmid b\), \(b\) has an inverse modulo every \(p^K\). The unique
residues solving \(br_K\equiv a\pmod{p^K}\) are compatible and determine
its digit expansion.

For example, the successive 2-adic residues of \(1/3\) at moduli
2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 are 1, 3, 3, 11 and 11. In series form,
\[
 \frac13=1+2+2^3+2^5+\cdots
       =1+2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}4^j.
\]
The geometric series converges because \(|4|_2<1\), and its sum gives
\(1+2/(1-4)=1/3\). The partial sum 11 is a poor real approximation of
\(1/3\), but \(\vp(11-1/3)=\vp(32/3)=5\): it is correct to five
2-adic digits. In \(\Z_3\), similarly,
\[
 \frac12=2+3+3^2+3^3+\cdots=(\ldots1112)_3,
\]
since \(2+3/(1-3)=1/2\) in the convergent 3-adic series.

A denominator containing \(p\) requires negative exponents instead.
For example, \(1/2=2^{-1}=(\ldots000.1)_2\) has valuation \(-1\)
and norm 2; \(1/3=3^{-1}\) has the analogous one-place representation
in \(\Q_3\). More generally, \(p^{-s}u\), with a unit \(u\in\Zp\),
shifts its expansion by \(s\) positions and has norm \(p^s\).
The radix point separates exponents 0 and \(-1\); it does not make such a
number small in the \(p\)-adic metric. Fractions therefore behave differently
for different primes even when their ordinary rational values are identical.

\subsection{Finite precision, balls and ordinary trees}

A computer stores only a finite object. For integer \(K\geq1\),
\[
 \RpK=\Zp/p^K\Zp\cong\Z/(p^K\Z)
\]
has \(p^K\) codes and retains digits \(a_0,\ldots,a_{K-1}\).
Reduction preserves ring addition and multiplication and commutes with
lower-precision reduction. A residue is invertible exactly when it is not
divisible by \(p\). For \(K>1\), this ring has zero divisors; it is neither
\(\Qp\) nor \(\F_p\). Division by a nonunit requires an explicit exponent
and precision convention, rather than an ordinary modular inverse.

For distinct finite residues, define \(d_K(a,b)=p^{-\vp(a-b)}\); set it
to zero at equality. The valuation below \(K\) is independent of the chosen
representatives. A depth-\(\ell\) ball is
\begin{equation}
 B_\ell(x,a)=\ind\{x\equiv a\pmod{p^\ell}\},\qquad0\leq\ell\leq K.
 \label{eq:ball}
\end{equation}
Reading low-power digits first makes this an ordinary radix prefix tree.
Identical labels give identical ball membership and lowest-common-ancestor
depth: a trie is an exact control for the geometry, not an approximation.
For \(m\) coordinates, the product metric
\(d_K^{(m)}(z,z')=\max_jd_K(z_j,z'_j)\) matches each coordinate's digits;
a next level can have \(p^m\) branches. Finite equality identifies an entire
ball of infinite lifts and is not infinite \(p\)-adic equality.
Unequal depths and exhausted-coordinate conventions are specified in SI
Section~\ref{si:precision}.

\subsection{Fixed residues and \texorpdfstring{\(p\)}{p}-adic floats}

Fixed absolute precision records \(x=\widetilde x+O(p^A)\), meaning that
the difference has valuation at least \(A\). For integral states this is
congruence modulo \(p^A\). A floating description instead records an exponent
and unit mantissa:
\[
 x=p^e\widetilde u+O(p^{e+N}),\qquad u\in\Zp^\times.
\]
Here \(N\) unit digits provide relative precision and \(e+N\) is absolute
precision. The exponent encodes divisibility. An implemented format still
needs exponent limits, normalization, rounding, zero conventions and
precision propagation; cancellation can change valuation
\citep{caruso2017computations}. Our controls implement fixed residues and
addresses, not \(p\)-adic floating-point arithmetic.

A real FP4 format attempts to approximate real magnitudes with a small
alphabet of signs, exponents and mantissas. A \(p\)-adic float approximates a different
metric, and a finite ternary digit is not automatically a BitNet ternary
weight. Neither the word ``float'' nor the number of symbols supplies a
quality or hardware comparison. Scales, real interfaces and accumulators
must be counted in the intended workload.

\subsection{Choosing a prime and connecting back to real values}

Binary residues allow masks and conventional packing; ternary and larger
primes change branching, units and reduction costs. A code requires at least
\(\lceil K\log_2p\rceil\) bits, before padding or metadata. Equal depth is
therefore unequal capacity, and equal entropy can still mean unequal actual
bytes. In our routing study the binary depths \((4,4)\) give 256 product
addresses and the ternary depths \((3,2)\) give 243 within one byte per
query-head address. The surrounding real projections and retained KV are
additional state. A coarsened ordinary binary hierarchy is essential to
separate a prime effect from a resolution effect.

Binary floats are dyadic rationals. Powers-of-two denominators are nonunits
for \(p=2\) but units for odd \(p\), as the fraction examples show. This
can matter for an exact modular equation, but does not make an odd-prime
residue a good real approximation of a neural weight. Clearing denominators
can change a finite-quotient problem when the multiplier is a nonunit
(SI Section~\ref{si:precision}).

The crucial obstruction is visible without a model:
\(p^N\to0\) \(p\)-adically while \(p^N\to\infty\) in real magnitude.
Hence small \(p\)-adic error gives no bound on real output error. A hybrid
component needs a specified encoder, operator and real decoder or score.
A digit-reversing semantic encoder may make common low-power digits mean
common coarse bins, but is not an embedding preserving real arithmetic.
Prime choice must follow this task and its measured implementation; our
experiments do not identify a universally preferable base.

\section{Why these structures could interest an LLM}
\label{sec:motivation}

\subsection{Hierarchical retrieval with real attention}

An address can encode a coarse category first and refinements later. Balls
then offer candidate sets at multiple resolutions, with explicit containment
and reproducible ties. A learned Q/K encoder could arrange important keys
near each query in this hierarchy. The potentially useful property is the
organization of retrieval, not divisibility of unencoded hidden vectors.
The central empirical test is whether the selected real keys retain enough
useful information at a worthwhile total cost.

Our implemented P2 uses calibrated, learned low-dimensional projections,
quantile bins and digit reversal. Binary or ternary product addresses route
candidates; the readout retains original real K/V, dot products and softmax.
SmolLM2-360M's first GQA layer has 15 query heads and five physical KV heads.
Each group of three query heads shares one KV head and selects the same
128 positions, including eight
mandatory recent positions. The precise shell-rank aggregation and newest-tie
rule are in SI Section~\ref{sec:shared-budget-design}. Aggregated head ranks
are a retrieval rule, not an ultrametric on the original neural states.

Nested buckets might permit cheaper lookup or selective fetch, but a code
scan still reads every older address. The exact causal prefix index reproduces
all 115,200 recorded fixture rows; that establishes selector equivalence,
not speed. Real KV remains resident and gathers, projections, lookup and
metadata must all be counted. Compression, evolving cached generation and
multilayer deployment require separate implementations and evidence.

\subsection{Multi-resolution features and structured learning}

A second candidate, P1, applies a finite-ring affine map
\(r=WE_K(h)+b\bmod p^K\), then exposes ball indicators
\(\phi_{i,\ell}=\ind\{r_i\equiv0\pmod{p^\ell}\}\) to a real readout.
Each coordinate supplies a nested feature bank; its sum across depths equals
\(\min(\vp(r_i),K)\), taking value \(K\) at zero. Such features could
share decisions across a hierarchy or model discrete structural attributes.
The small binary recipe has now been tested by teacher-output ridge fitting
and exact digit proposals. It fails the development progression criteria;
this bounds the tested learner rather than all ball-feature architectures.
The construction and complete pilot are in SI Sections~\ref{si:p1}
and~\ref{app:ball-ffn-results}.

Reduction \(\pi_k:\Z/(p^K\Z)\to\Z/(p^k\Z)\), \(k\leq K\),
preserves addition and multiplication. A decision factoring through \(\pi_k\)
is invariant under changes by multiples of \(p^k\). These exact contracts
concern encoded states; they do not guarantee robustness of a real LLM
encoder. Similarly, aggregating a fine probability distribution over prefixes
is consistent across resolutions, while fine and coarse maximum-probability
decisions can disagree. SI Section~\ref{app:semantic-structure} derives these
contracts and tests an independently labelled semantic hierarchy on frozen
LLM features. Ordinary trees and flat probability aggregation share the
corresponding guarantees; task quality needs a separate comparison.

\subsection{Testing an existing model before a new architecture}

A frozen pretrained model permits a localized intervention and a matched
full readout on identical tokens. Fit an encoder or real readout on separate
adaptation data, fix its resolutions and quotas on development documents,
and evaluate hard inference on untouched documents in all fixed training
seeds. Compare dense, locality, coverage and ordinary hierarchy controls
before attributing any benefit to the prime. This is the route taken here;
training a new whole LLM is not needed to test this component hypothesis.

A full \(p\)-adic neural architecture would additionally need nonlinearities,
normalization, training and a real probability interface appropriate to its
field. Exact residue arithmetic alone does not solve those problems.
A conceptual claim requires a specified mechanism, adequate adaptation and
task-quality evidence against matched controls. Hardware gains require a
separate cost comparison of the same executable operator and workload;
they are not a prerequisite for a useful representation or learning result.

\section{Central experiments and findings}
\label{sec:main-results}

\subsection{Arithmetic controls delimit the claim}

The synthetic control and frozen FFN down-projection interventions on
SmolLM2-135M/360M show the expected null: sufficiently wide binary residues
reproduce ordinary quantized integer computation exactly. The balanced
reconstruction of a dot sum \(S\) is exact if \(|S|\leq A\) and \(p^K>2A\).
Narrow wrapping changes the real output and can severely damage likelihood;
in the 135M control, roughly 1.21\% wrapped dots at 12 bits accompany PPL
above 461, versus about 26.53 for ordinary W4/A8 reconstruction.
The implementation is replicated on Spark GB10 and Terok A40, without a
native speed comparison. All arithmetic, projection and model-size results
are in SI Sections~\ref{si:arithmetic} and~\ref{si:components}.

\subsection{Learned routing and the untouched WikiText screen}

Three output-supervised real dual-affine Q/K bridges are fitted on separate
512-token training articles in frozen SmolLM2-360M. Development compares
hard selectors on 43 separate articles at 512/2,048 tokens. Ternary has
smaller point loss than binary in every longer-context seed, but uniform
coverage and ordinary coarsened binary are stronger still. Retained teacher
mass, output error and token loss have different rankings. The full development
sequence and all 29 readouts are retained in SI Section~\ref{si:components}.

The untouched WikiText test retains all 40 eligible articles, each at 2,048
tokens, with 81,880 scored next-token targets. Original weights, three bridge
fits, 128-position group budgets and tie rules are unchanged. The two
endpoints are all targets and affected prediction positions 128--2,046.
For paired losses, define
\[
 \Delta=\NLL_{\rm candidate}-\NLL_{\rm reference},\qquad
 \PPL=\exp(\NLL),\qquad\delta=\log(1.01).
\]
Each family must have its 97.5\% upper bound strictly below \(\delta\) for
every seed, reference (stock, uniform, same-seed coarsened binary) and
endpoint. We use 20,000 paired whole-document resamples, recomputing total
token-weighted losses. Bootstrap coverage is approximate, and inference
concerns these three fixed fits rather than a population of training seeds.
This is a prespecified quality allowance, not a hardware saving.

Both families pass all 18 required bounds. The largest stock-reference
affected-target upper PPL changes are 0.77443\% for binary and 0.52253\%
for ternary. Positive point loss increases remain compatible with this
non-inferiority result; ordinary controls have smaller point losses.
Numerical and independently implemented statistical verification pass.
Every readout and primary comparison appears in SI
Tables~\ref{tab:heldout-all} and~\ref{tab:heldout-primary}.

\subsection{The distinct native PG-19 operator fails its quality screen}

A separate, prospectively frozen study selects 40 of 99 eligible PG-19 test
books using a fixed permutation \citep{pg19data}. Nested 2,048/4,096-token
prefixes yield 80 windows and 245,680 scored targets. The bridge fits stay
fixed, but native single-query encoding and Flash selected-key readout operate
on original uncached full-sequence Q/K/V. This first-layer quality evaluation
is distinct from cached full-model generation and from the eager WikiText
operator. Corpus, context and operator change together, so the result cannot
isolate a single cause of transfer failure.

all \(p\)-adic approaches.

\subsection{Finite-ring FFN learning pilot}

The binary P1 pilot uses a training-only 16-coordinate real encoder,
32 ring-affine rows, 128 ball features and a ridge-fitted real readout.
Across three fixed seeds, two hard digit-search sweeps accept no ring changes.
Complete first-layer FFN replacement increases reused-development NLL by
7.081--7.154 nats/target; ordinary threshold controls are stronger, though
also unsuitable replacements. All progression criteria fail jointly.
This bounds the small architecture and frozen-readout learner, without
separating their causes or deciding other \(p\)-adic designs. Complete controls,
losses and independent checks are in SI Section~\ref{app:ball-ffn-results}.

\subsection{Semantic structure learns, but does not improve unseen transfer}

A separate CLINC150 probe uses frozen LLM features and an independently
annotated domain/intent hierarchy \citep{larson2019clinc}. Twenty examples
from each of 140 intents train real heads; one complete intent per domain
is withheld. All three semantic-prefix fits learn and have 65.77--65.82\%
seen-intent accuracy versus 39.47--42.81\% for label-shuffled identical trees.
This is a descriptive semantic-topology effect. A supervision- and
parameter-matched flat classifier is stronger at 77.68--77.73\%.
On 300 unseen-intent utterances, domain accuracy is 69\% for semantic
prefixes, 74\% for flat and 83.33\% for a smaller direct-domain classifier.
Every primary progression rule fails. The real prefix model equals ordinary
hierarchical softmax \citep{morin2005hierarchical}; this classification
result is neither generative LM non-inferiority nor a uniquely \(p\)-adic
advantage. Complete outcomes and verified path contracts are in SI
Section~\ref{app:semantic-structure}.

\subsection{Secondary hardware characterization on Spark}

The independent component-cost plan includes all 13 variants across six
fixed prefix workloads. All 78 correctness cases pass, including exact
codes, latents, IDs and BF16 head/projected outputs, and an optimized
contiguous recency-view control. The first allocation closes before timing
because of observed foreign CUDA work. A separate prospective Spark allocation
then completes all 378,000 samples and independent timing/storage verification,
with every method, seed and workload retained. Complete finite calls are
slower and use more state than matched full Flash in this warm replay.
These are costs of the current implementation, not a test that can reject
all \(p\)-adic concepts. The complete measurement scope is in SI
Sections~\ref{sec:native-cost} and~\ref{app:native-cost}.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

The promising mathematical property is a controllable hierarchy with exact
finite-resolution contracts. Its value depends on what an encoder makes
nearby and on whether retrieval or representation improves a measured
tradeoff. Ordinary trie equivalence constrains novelty without preventing
an application from being useful. Our development results show that
resolution, tie-induced locality and physical GQA sharing deserve explicit
controls; smaller ternary point losses do not identify a universal prime.

The evidence is limited to small models from one family and localized
first-layer interventions. Early pilots reuse development data; the two
untouched screens support only their declared operators and workloads.
Forty document units, fixed seeds and approximate bootstrap bounds limit
uncertainty inference. Exact text exclusions do not rule out semantic overlap
or model-pretraining exposure. A simultaneous change of corpus, context and
operator prevents causal attribution of the native transfer failure.

All original real KV remains resident. Candidate counts, encoder bytes and
prefix-index equivalence are not hardware latency or cache compression.
The completed component benchmark establishes no resource advantage for this
implementation. It cannot isolate a limitation of GPU hardware from the
real encoder, full-prefix selection and unfused execution. Nor does it
predict another implementation or future hardware. The P1 pilot also fails
to adapt its hard ring parameters or retain useful replacement quality;
encoder capacity, feature count and the frozen-readout search are not isolated.
The semantic probe confirms a hierarchy-versus-shuffling effect but no benefit
over stronger flat controls or on its unseen-intent task. Its binary
intermediate splits and real parameterization limit attribution.
The continuous-disk branch learns a certified modular rule on typed operands,
but its fixed real logit fusion fails the Fourier comparison. Equivalent
ordinary congruence arithmetic reproduces its scores; the positive learned
rule and failed answer fusion answer different questions.
Other P1 learners, evolving cached generation and broader model coverage remain
prospective. The SI distinguishes completed evidence
from these open requirements (Section~\ref{si:evaluation}). The selected
nearest-work audit also does not prove exhaustive novelty.

\section{Current conclusion}

Finite \(p\)-adic objects offer explicit arithmetic and hierarchical
constructions, but the metric cannot substitute for real neural accuracy.
Our verified controls establish conventional arithmetic equivalences;
the learned routing component passes one frozen first-layer quality screen
and fails a separately frozen native transfer screen. A small ring/ball FFN
fits an informative real readout but fails hard-parameter adaptation and
replacement quality. A learned semantic-prefix classifier improves on shuffled
hierarchies for seen intents but fails its unseen-intent progression rule.
A new continuous-disk branch learns exact addition-modulo rules, while
its fixed LLM-prior fusion fails the competitive answer gate.
These are scoped outcomes, not a hardware verdict.
The routing result is limited
feasibility evidence for the concrete hybrid, with unresolved transfer and
no uniquely \(p\)-adic quality advantage over stronger ordinary controls.
Secondary costs rule out an efficiency claim for the measured implementation,
without deciding whether another \(p\)-adic component is useful. The conceptual
question must be tested through specified encoders, resolutions, tasks and
matched ablations; hardware optimization is a separate subsequent question.

\section{Extended related work and claim boundaries}
\label{si:related}

\(p\)-adic neural models predate present language models
\citep{albeverio1999networks}. Baker and Molla applied a 2-adic regression
formulation to noun pluralisation \citep{baker2022pluralisation}. Martins
develops classification, regression, and semantic-network constructions while
identifying substantial open learning questions \citep{martins2025learning}.
Mihara uses \(p\)-adic characters as activations and reduces finite approximation
problems to polynomial feasibility \citep{mihara2026character}.
Ball-indicator representation learning is investigated by v-PuNNs
\citep{nguessan2025vpunn}; non-Archimedean tree-like neural architectures with
real-valued approximation results also exist \citep{zuniga2026deep}.
These works motivate investigation but do not establish the performance of the
components proposed here.

Finite \(p\)-adic implementations and precision analysis are established
numerical mathematics \citep{caruso2017computations}. Modular arithmetic is also
used in neural computation outside a \(p\)-adic geometric interpretation, for
example in RNS Winograd convolution \citep{liu2020rns}. A currently indexed HiLD
manuscript studies modular stationarity systems and lifting
\citep{padiclowbit2026}. Its author identities, final publication status, and
full experimental tables have not been verified in this study; no precise
empirical conclusion from that manuscript is used here.

MoBA selects contiguous blocks through query dot products with mean keys,
forces causal current-block attention and combines block outputs by online
softmax \citep{lu2025moba}. Its reported large-model downstream evaluation
uses sparse prefill but full attention during generation. NSA combines
trained compression, group-shared original-token block selection and a
separate local branch, with learned gates and native kernels
\citep{yuan2025nsa}. HiP uses representative native scores in greedy
chronological chunk refinement; its analysis depends on locality and
representative-dependence assumptions \citep{lee2025hip}. Thus group sharing,
hierarchical pruning and pretrained adaptation are established mechanisms.
These original methods were inspected but their performance was not replicated;
the frozen first-layer experiment does not reproduce their complete systems.

Strong conversion baselines extend beyond uniform rounding.
CAT-Q investigates learned ternary conversion \citep{wang2026catq}; QuIP\#
and AQLM use vector or additive codebooks \citep{tseng2024quip,egiazarian2024aqlm}.
Native low-bit training and post-training conversion must be distinguished:
different pretrained checkpoints do not constitute a matched causal comparison.

For attention, content-based clustering and hierarchical document processing
are established approaches \citep{roy2021routing,he2024hdt}.
Quest performs query-aware KV-page selection, SnapKV selects important cached
positions, and KIVI compresses KV tensors through asymmetric quantization
\citep{tang2024quest,li2024snapkv,liu2024kivi}. The proposed index must be assessed
against these mechanisms, not only against dense attention.

Closer index-based precedents further narrow the possible contribution.
ZETA learns low-dimensional Q/K projections and a binary Z-order index for
causal candidate search, together with an Adaptive Cauchy attention kernel
\citep{zeng2025zeta}. Its scalar Z-order neighborhood differs from the product
ultrametric used here, but learned radix addresses alone are not a new idea.
MiniMax Sparse Attention learns a lightweight block index shared within GQA
groups \citep{lai2026msa}. Hierarchical Global Attention proposes hierarchical
routing for frozen pretrained Transformers while reading the original selected
token K/V states \citep{hga2026routing}; exact selected-token readout does not
mean equality to dense attention. ZETA is published at ICLR 2025; the latter
two sources are June 2026 preprints whose accepted venues have not been verified
in this audit. Their performance claims have not been reproduced here.
A defensible prospective question therefore concerns measured prime, precision,
encoding and physical GQA-access tradeoffs against matched flat and hierarchical
controls, rather than inventing hierarchical or group-aware attention.

Public, unreviewed repositories already implement ultrametric attention, prefix
aggregation, and pretrained LLM topology injection. A read-only audit now pins
five repositories and inspects their concrete mechanisms
(SI Section~\ref{app:public-code}); their quantitative and formal-verification
claims remain unreplicated. Consequently, this manuscript does not claim the first
\(p\)-adic attention mechanism, the first tree-based neural representation,
or novelty of radix-tree evaluation. Mathematical equivalence to a conventional
tree remains compatible with a useful application contribution; it constrains
the wording of that contribution.

A further pinned source review inspects SSA's real center/radius trees,
causal GQA routing and separate attention-error certificates, and POET's
published model metadata (SI Section~\ref{app:certified-code}). These sources
also preclude generic novelty claims for certified geometric sparsity;
their reported performance is not our experimental evidence.


\section{Additional finite-precision contracts}
\label{si:precision}

The derivation, negative/fractional examples, quotient ring, unit condition
and fixed/floating descriptions are given in main-text
Section~\ref{sec:theory}. The following implementation contracts supplement
that construction.

\subsection{Unequal coordinate resolutions}

Unequal coordinate resolutions give the product
\(\prod_j R_{p,K_j}\), with metric \(\max_j d_{K_j}\). An equal finite coordinate
has distance zero and imposes no further constraint after its digits are
exhausted. Tree implementations use a fixed sentinel for exhausted coordinates;
at level \(\ell\), only coordinates with \(K_j>\ell\) can add branches.
This convention is necessary when matching a byte budget with an unequal
digit allocation, as in the prime comparison below.

\subsection{Packing, capacity and experimental scope}

Prime comparisons must state both effective code capacity and actual bytes.
A single residue needs at least \(\lceil K\log_2p\rceil\) bits; an implementation
can use more through padding, per-digit packing, or metadata. In particular,
storing every ternary digit in two bits is different from packing a whole
radix-3 code. The synthetic sweep below uses a common entropy cap only.
Confirmatory component experiments will compare \(p=2,3\) and the \(p=5\) control
at equal measured serialized-byte budgets, reporting leftover capacity and
all auxiliary state. The LLM arithmetic controls use \(p=2\); the offline routing diagnostic now
compares all three primes at equal address bytes, but unequal encoder overhead.

\subsection{Nonunit denominator clearing and lifting}

Clearing denominators requires care. Multiplying a rational equation by a
common denominator preserves its exact rational solutions, but a nonunit
factor can create spurious solutions in a finite quotient. For example,
\(2(x-1)=0\) modulo 2 holds for every residue, whereas \(x-1=0\) holds only at 1.
Record valuations, precision loss, and verification in the original equations;
do not treat cleared modular systems as equivalent without these checks.
Standard simple-root Hensel lifting uses a unit derivative, or an appropriate
invertible Jacobian in a system. It is not a general convergence guarantee for
neural-network optimization \citep{caruso2017computations,padiclowbit2026}.


\section{Exact modular computation as a control}
\label{si:arithmetic}

\subsection{Integer recovery and binary equivalence}

Let \(q_i,r_i\) be signed integer codes and
\[
 S=\sum_{i=1}^{d}q_i r_i,\qquad M=p^K.
\]
Choose the balanced decoder \(D_M\) with interval
\[
 I_M=\{-\lfloor M/2\rfloor,\ldots,\lceil M/2\rceil-1\}.
\]
For even \(M\), \(+M/2\) decodes to \(-M/2\).

\begin{proposition}[Bounded integer recovery]
If \(|S|\leq A\) and \(M>2A\), then \(D_M(S\bmod M)=S\).
In particular, \(A=\sum_i|q_i||r_i|\) is sufficient.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The strict bound places \(S\) in \(I_M\), whose elements are unique
representatives modulo \(M\). The bound on the sum follows from the
ordinary triangle inequality.
\end{proof}

For W4 codes in \([-7,7]\) and A8 codes in \([-127,127]\), a sufficient
worst-case condition is
\[
 M>2d\cdot7\cdot127.
\]
Small weights do not imply a similarly small accumulator. When \(M=2^K\),
the residue computation and balanced decoder are exactly signed
two's-complement wrapping accumulation, including wraparound.
Without wraparound the reconstruction is the conventional quantized integer
result; with wraparound it is a different real output. Neither case
establishes an intrinsic \(p\)-adic numerical improvement.

If real scales \(s_w,s_x\) remain outside the ring, the reconstructed
output is \(s_ws_xD_M(S\bmod M)\). This is a hybrid operation. The scales,
conversion, and decoder remain part of its implementation cost.

\subsection{P1: finite-ring affine and ball-indicator FFN}
\label{si:p1}

For a real hidden state \(h\in\R^d\), define an encoder
\(E_K:\R^d\to R_{p,K}^{m}\), finite-ring weights \(W,b\), and
\[
 z=E_K(h),\qquad r=Wz+b\pmod{p^K}.
\]
For \(n\) ring coordinates, construct multi-depth features
\begin{equation}
 \phi_{i,\ell}=\ind\{r_i\equiv0\pmod{p^\ell}\},
 \quad i=1,\ldots,n,\quad \ell=1,\ldots,K,
 \qquad f(h)=C\phi+c\in\R^d .
 \label{eq:ffn}
\end{equation}
Here \(C,c\) are real readout parameters. The sum over depths satisfies
\(\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\phi_{i,\ell}=\min(\vp(r_i),K)\), with the sum equal
to \(K\) at residue zero. Thus the feature bank exposes capped valuation
information without claiming infinite precision.

The tested small binary configuration is \(p=2,m=16,n=32,K=4\).
This gives 512 ring weights and 128 indicator features before the readout.
Any later \(p=3\) or \(p=5\) FFN comparison requires a separate capacity and
actual-byte design; those primes are not tested as LLM FFNs here.
The real readout dominates this pilot's stored arrays; its cost and that of
the real projection in \(E_K\) are reported in
Section~\ref{app:ball-ffn-results} rather than attributed to the ring alphabet.

The pilot begins with a frozen teacher and disjoint adaptation activations.
With encoder and ring parameters fixed, fit the readout to teacher FFN
outputs using ridge regression. Then alternate
readout updates with exact discrete coordinate/digit proposals for \(W,b\).
Proposal evaluations and teacher access count towards training cost.
A hard-forward surrogate-gradient variant may be evaluated separately;
it is a heuristic for a discrete model, not a \(p\)-adic derivative.

An added branch and an FFN replacement answer different questions.
A zero-initialized added branch preserves the base model but does not initially
save FFN bytes. Compression claims require replacement and full cost accounting.
Controls include a matched real bottleneck FFN, a modular affine module with
signed readout, unmixed ball features, randomized hierarchy labels, and a tree
implementation of identical features. The completed fixed-learner pilot is
reported in Section~\ref{app:ball-ffn-results}; useful hard-ring adaptation and
replacement quality remain unestablished.

\subsection{P2: causal hierarchical KV routing}

P2 uses finite \(p\)-adic codes as an index while preserving real keys, values,
dot-product attention, and softmax in the initial study. Given post-transform
query/key vectors, including the model's positional transformations, apply
shared calibrated projections. Quantize each projection into \(p^K\) ordered
bins. Reverse the significance of its root-to-leaf radix-\(p\) digits to obtain
a residue whose low-power digits encode coarse bins. The initial binary
implementation uses \(p=2,m=2,K=4\); its eight-bit leaf address does not make
the full KV state an eight-bit representation. Ternary and \(p=5\) address
variants are implemented in the offline diagnostic below. End-to-end conversion
and matching the full component's measured bytes remain open.

Maintain an append-only causal index of key codes. At a query, select keys
from one or more balls or prefix shells, with depth and candidate budget chosen
on development data. Compute the original real attention over those candidates,
respecting the causal mask. Local/recency candidates, empty buckets, boundaries,
and full-attention fallback must have fixed rules. Router training, if used,
will optimize a stated real objective such as retained teacher attention mass.

The geometry is exact for the codes; it need not approximate pretrained
query/key dot-product similarity. Recall of important attention mass is therefore
a diagnostic to be measured, not a property to assume. Multiple tables or
fallbacks can improve recall while consuming savings.

Required controls are an identical radix trie, equal-budget projection hashing,
flat clustering, random-plus-local selection, relevant KV-selection methods,
and the dense pretrained model. A scan over every stored code can validate
membership but cannot establish efficient indexing. Costs include projection,
encoding, insertion, lookup, gather/scatter, selected attention, and fallback.
If all K/V remain resident, KV storage is unchanged plus index overhead.
Eviction or prototype compression is a separate design.
A first-layer P2 language-model pilot is reported below. Broader model-quality
confirmation and native-runtime results remain pending.

\subsection{Shared-budget and context-extension development}
\label{sec:shared-budget-design}

A separate frozen development gate completes all 86 model windows and full
separate result verification passes. The three final encoders and
their books remain unchanged. All 43 eligible validation articles with at
least 2,048 tokens supply their first 512 and first 2,048 tokens separately,
with the previous article-level exclusions. An independent delimiter/token
check verifies all 86 nested windows before forwarding: 110,080 input tokens
and 109,994 next-token targets. These correlated prefixes are development
history, not independent confirmation. Training remains restricted to the
original 512-token windows, so this measures context transfer without refitting.

Each group of three query heads now shares exactly the same causal set of
128 selected positions, including the latest eight. On the optional older
pool, each head's score receives twice its midrank,
\[
 r_{hj}=2\#\{k:s_{hk}<s_{hj}\}+\#\{k:s_{hk}=s_{hj}\}-1.
\]
The fixed group score is \(R_j=\sum_{h\in G}r_{hj}\); aggregate ties prefer
newer keys. This changed rank-aggregating router gives heads equal influence
without comparing raw score scales. It is not the earlier per-head selector
or a newly trained ultrametric. All original real-dot-product readouts and
K/V remain; shared selection is not measured native physical I/O.

In particular, the group score is a query-pool-dependent retrieval order,
not a distance asserted to be ultrametric on neural states. Sums do not
generally preserve ultrametricity: let \(d_1\) be the discrete distance on
\(R_{2,1}\), and let \(D\) sum it over two coordinates. For
\(x=(0,0)\), \(y=(1,0)\), and \(z=(1,1)\),
\[
 D(x,y)=D(y,z)=1,\qquad D(x,z)=2.
\]
This violates the strong triangle inequality. The example concerns a sum
of genuine distances, not our midrank formula; no metric guarantee follows
for that formula either. Distinct learned query/key maps also do not define
one common metric on the original real states. The finite congruence geometry
is exact for the addresses, while the aggregate ordering is an explicit
empirical design.

The 29 declared readouts comprise full attention, recency, four sinks plus
recency, uniform older-key coverage and the summed native group-mass upper
control; each seed contributes binary/ternary balls, a coarsened binary
hierarchy, ordinary grids using the identical binary/ternary quantile
partitions, flat centroids, Gaussian selection, and a fixed 64-local/64-global
Gaussian control. The grids invert digit reversal and rank normalized real
bin-ordinal distances. The coarsened hierarchy retains binary depths 0, 2

The group policy also has a simple score-alphabet bound. For a fixed optional
pool of \(n\) keys, if head \(h\) has \(a_h\) score-tie classes, the summed
midrank has at most \(\min\{n,\prod_{h\in G}a_h\}\) distinct values: each key
belongs to one tuple of headwise tie classes, and a tuple determines its sum.
Distinct tuples may share a sum. Binary depths 0--4 and ternary depths 0--3
thus give upper bounds 125 and 64 for three heads, whereas coarsened binary
gives 27. Counts and ties, rather than the prime alone, determine the actual
alphabet. Histogram-based exact selection is a systems option, but obtaining
joint memberships, newest-key ties and grouped K/V still requires work. This
counting argument supplies no sublinear-index, native-speed or novelty claim.

\subsection{Exact causal prefix-index implementation}
\label{sec:causal-index}

A separate CPU correctness gate implements the finite group selector without
scoring every older key by default. Each head stores causal ancestor posting
lists for its packed addresses. Subtree counts give exact shell multiplicities
and the twice-midranks defined above. Each head exposes keys in decreasing
rank, then decreasing timestamp; newly exposed distinct candidates receive
their full three-head score and update a top-120 heap. The recent eight
positions remain mandatory outside the index. This applies conventional
sorted/random-access threshold aggregation \citep{fagin2003aggregation}; it
does not introduce a new aggregation algorithm or an intrinsic arithmetic
advantage.

Let the latest exposed item in head \(h\) be \((a_h,t_h)\). Every unseen key
has summed rank at most \(\sum_h a_h\). Equality requires its rank to equal
every head's frontier rank, so its timestamp is at most \(\min_h t_h\).
Consequently the lexicographic bound
\[
 U=\left(\sum_h a_h,\min_h t_h\right)
\]
certifies exact termination when the heap's least retained score/timestamp
pair is at least \(U\). Exhausting one head list exposes every older key.
Thus newest-key tie resolution is included in the certificate. A query may
still visit every key; no sublinear worst-case or instance-optimality claim is
made for this implementation and cost model.

The frozen fixtures are the first eligible development article at both full
512/2,048-token lengths, every seed and binary/ternary/coarsened-binary
recipe. All 115,200 complete group-mask rows match the independently verified
dense development masks exactly, including early full-context queries;
causality, cardinality and recent-eight membership are checked on every row.
All affected queries terminate with the score/timestamp certificate. Four
additional synthetic tests pass. There is no new fitting, model forward or
held-out quality selection.

\subsection{Native decode gate: a failed address identity}
\label{sec:native-identity}

A separate frozen GPU correctness gate implements compact-code lookup,
integer shell histograms, exact twice-midranks and score/timestamp top-$k$,
then gathers each shared K/V list once per physical group. It uses original
BF16 Q/K/V, all three final seeds and both complete first-development-article
contexts. Each new query/key is encoded through a fifteen-head single-token
FP32 batched affine operation and the unchanged FP64 PCA/quantile book;
TF32 is disabled. This is a different numerical contraction from the archived
per-head block operation, even though the real affine formulas agree.

The declared requirement is exact equality of all 460,800 query/key code
entries and all 115,200 group-mask rows. It fails: ten code entries differ,
representing five unique addresses repeated in nested prefixes. Only ternary
seed 17 changes masks, with 63 rows at 512 and 315 at 2,048 tokens; the other
114,822 rows match. A separate CPU audit, importing no production modules,
reconstructs every saved address difference and all implied mask counts and
examples by direct congruences, sorting and insertion ranks. It does not
claim a second complete replay of GPU masks, which were not saved in full.

Targeted post-gate witnesses reproduce all ten changes. In each case the
archived real projection lies exactly on a quantile cutpoint, whereas the
single-token contraction falls just below it. The five distinct signed
crossings range from $-5.4063\times10^{-9}$ to
$-2.0561\times10^{-7}$ in projected coordinates. The changed seed-17 ternary
key is head 14, position 32; its packed address changes from 65 to 200 and
propagates through later selections in group 4. The packed integer jump is
not a real-distance error measure. No cutpoint, precision, seed or quota is
adjusted to rescue the frozen identity.

Independently, all 72 predeclared one-query readouts pass their selected-set
FP64 arithmetic tolerances. These span stock, recency, uniform and all nine
finite seed/recipe pairs at half, three-quarter and complete prefixes for
both contexts. Forced PyTorch \texttt{FLASH\_ATTENTION} supports every probed
shape; no math fallback is used. There are five physical KV heads and fifteen
query heads. The supplied cache prefix is already causal, so
\texttt{is\_causal=False}, \texttt{enable\_gqa=True} and zero dropout are
explicit; one-query upper-left causal masking would expose only the first
key \cite{pytorchsdpa}. Maximum head NRMSE is 0.0026054 and projected NRMSE
0.0052307, below the fixed 0.01/0.015 limits; all fixed maximum-absolute-error
bounds also pass. The separate CPU FP64 reconstruction verifies all saved
readouts and metrics within $6.22\times10^{-15}$.

This is a failed numerical-identity result, not a quality failure measured
by NLL. Sparse integer selection and selected-set attention agreement cannot
make a changed discrete encoder the previously confirmed operator. Native
latency measurements are therefore not started under this identity gate,
and the held-out quality screen below is not transferred to this GPU path.
A native encoding contract requires its own frozen quality validation before
a resource claim. The gate and all failure artifacts are preserved in
\texttt{results/\allowbreak{}native-\allowbreak{}decode-\allowbreak{}correctness-\allowbreak{}spark-\allowbreak{}20261004};
the independent diagnostic audit and targeted cutpoint witnesses are separate
archives. Original real KV remains resident, and no complete-model generation
or cache-eviction measurement is made.


\section{Full quality results, statistical design and open gates}
\label{si:evaluation}

\subsection{Conceptual outcomes and statistical design}

The primary task-quality endpoint and proposed structural mechanism must be
fixed for each confirmatory experiment. A routing claim can concern useful
selected information and model NLL against locality, coverage and resolution
controls; an FFN claim can concern learnable ball features or modular
structure against matched ordinary features. A useful resource target is
separately required if the claim concerns efficiency. For example, that
additional claim can concern decoding latency at a fixed workload and
acceptable quality margin, or token NLL at equal total serialized bytes.
Quality, storage, resident memory, training effort, and latency describe
different tradeoffs and must not be selected opportunistically after testing.

For a candidate and matched comparator, define
\[
 \Delta=\NLL_{\mathrm{candidate}}-\NLL_{\mathrm{reference}},
 \qquad \PPL=e^{\NLL}.
\]
A permitted perplexity ratio \(\rho\) corresponds to the NLL margin
\(\delta=\log\rho\). The initial broad protocol proposed \(\rho=1.05\)
as a starting design choice. A separate held-out first-layer quality protocol
now freezes \(\rho=1.01\), adopting the earlier routing-specific proposal
before reading official-test text or outputs. The allowance screens a
128/2,048 logical-position budget; it is not a community standard, a hardware
saving or an achieved result. Both whole-window and affected-target endpoints
are required, so unchanged early targets alone cannot satisfy it. This rule
must not be applied retroactively to classify the development pilots.
Non-inferiority requires the upper one-sided confidence bound on \(\Delta\)
to lie below \(\delta\). A nonsignificant difference is insufficient.

Use document-paired loss measurements with identical scored-token masks.
Bootstrap whole documents where justified, recomputing total token-weighted
NLL, rather than treating correlated tokens as independent observations.
Trained candidates require repeated seeds. The frozen held-out gate retains
all three existing states; no best seed is selected, and inference over all
possible training seeds is not claimed. Document resampling cannot replace
training-seed replication.

The frozen quality gate uses all eligible official WikiText test articles
of at least 2,048 tokens, with complete train/validation text-hash duplicates
and repeated test articles excluded. Article inventories and exact token
prefixes must be frozen and independently verified before any forward.
It reuses all 29 readouts and unchanged 512-trained routing states. Stock,
uniform coverage and the same-seed coarsened binary hierarchy are required
comparators. Its complete 62-article inventory is now frozen and independently
verified: all 40 articles of at least 2,048 tokens are eligible, supplying
81,880 scored targets and 76,760 affected targets at prediction positions
128--2,046. All 40 model windows, full numerical verification and independent
statistical verification are complete. Results follow the frozen rule below.

At least 40 eligible article units are required for a quality classification.
The fixed procedure uses 20,000 paired document bootstrap resamples, PCG64
seed 514203, recomputes token-weighted loss sums, and takes the linearly
interpolated 97.5th percentile as the upper bound. Each binary/ternary family
must pass against all three comparators, in all three fixed seeds, for both
all-target and affected-target losses. This intersection rule and two candidate
families provide a nominal 5\% familywise screening allocation if individual
bounds have their stated coverage; bootstrap coverage is approximate, not
exact finite-sample control. Report failures, inconclusive bounds and all
secondary readouts. Passing this quality gate does not establish a
competitive resource operating point. The frozen protocol is
\texttt{research/\allowbreak{}heldout-\allowbreak{}quality-\allowbreak{}protocol.\allowbreak{}md}; this separate
previously unread-test gate has a 10,800-second cumulative guard and does not extend
any development guard.

\subsection{Evidence required before a completed paper}

\begin{enumerate}
\item A precise finite object and executable operator, with ring, ball,
precision, decoding and exact-equivalence tests.
\item A defensible contribution relative to nearest mathematical, learned
routing, KV-selection and public-code work.
\item At least one implemented meaningful candidate beyond R0, with adequate
adaptation and hard-inference evaluation.
\item Matched baselines, replicated uncertainty, raw per-example records and
predeclared quality/mechanism endpoints, including failures. Resource endpoints
are separately needed for any efficiency claim.
\item Replication across another model, corpus or workload appropriate to the
generality of the claimed outcome.
\item Transparent secondary cost accounting, reproducible artifacts, and a compiled,
internally consistent manuscript whose abstract and conclusion match the evidence.
\end{enumerate}

The primary question concerns conceptual usefulness; a speedup is not a
condition for accepting a useful representation or learning result.
This framing changes no frozen protocol, quality threshold or outcome.
Broader conceptual attribution and transfer remain open. If a candidate
fails, the conclusion must identify its encoding, training, resolution,
workload and comparator rather than generalize to all \(p\)-adic approaches.


\section{Detailed limitations and evidence boundaries}

The synthetic Gaussian pilot does not reproduce Transformer activation
statistics and matches an entropy cap, not packed bytes or throughput.
The frozen-projection controls use two sizes from one small model family, only
the first FFN down-projection, one ordered validation excerpt and short separate
contexts. Cross-host replication checks
implementation behavior, not statistical generality. The offline routing
study compares three primes in one layer of the same development excerpt;
neither retained mass nor value-output error establishes language-model quality.
The masked-attention pilots supply actual token NLL at two model sizes,
including two-system replication of the smaller model, but only for frozen
first-layer interventions and reused development data. Their small differences
have no confirmatory uncertainty or resource benefit attached.
Counts contain repeated and correlated evaluations, not independent examples.
Secondary native component costs have now been measured for their own
specified warm workload; they establish no efficiency gain.

The learned Q/K bridge has both training-fit and hard-selection development
evidence on separate articles, with all three fixed seeds. Binary adaptation
improves over its own initial states, and ternary losses are close to fixed
locality controls, but extra routing state and GQA unions remain. The shared-position-budget gate supplies stronger ordinary controls and
2,048-token context transfer. The subsequent untouched article-level screen
supports both finite families within its declared quality allowance; native
broader coverage remains required. It establishes
neither compression nor a competitive native resource operating point.

The separately frozen native PG-19 screen is fully verified and fails for
both finite families, including every full-reference bound at 4,096 tokens.
Ternary has smaller point loss than binary in each fixed seed/context, but
ordinary recency, uniform and coarsened-binary controls remain stronger.
The simultaneous operator/corpus/context change prevents isolating a cause;
the result is confined to this exact trained bridge and routing contract.
The first component-cost allocation closes before timing. Its separate
prospective Spark follow-up passes all 78 cases and complete independent
timing/storage verification, preserving both histories. The finite complete
calls are slower and retain more state than matched full in this warm replay.
This is an implementation result, not a negative decision on all \(p\)-adic
components or evidence that hardware support alone caused the costs.

Further P1 training, broad P2 evaluation and evolving cached generation remain
prospective; the small fixed P1 learner is evaluated in
Section~\ref{app:ball-ffn-results}. Their real interfaces
may dominate cost, their encodings may fail to retain useful semantics, and
discrete training may not scale. P2 may miss large dot-product contributions
across bin boundaries; fallback can erase an apparent sparsity advantage.
P1 can produce collapsed ball features, especially when nonunit coefficients
erase digits. These are mechanisms to diagnose, not measured failures.

The broader literature and final contribution audit remain incomplete. The
pinned nearest-code inspection already precludes several first-ever claims. A tree-equivalent application may
still be scientifically useful, but any observed benefit must be attributed
to the actual hierarchy, learning rule, precision behavior or implementation.

The completed controls confirm that finite residue arithmetic behaves as
specified, that binary wrapping is its exact conventional counterpart, and
that narrow reconstruction can strongly damage a frozen model's real-valued
likelihood. The routing diagnostic confirms exact prefix-tree equivalence and mixed quality
proxies. The masked-attention pilot extends this equivalence to actual model
outputs and token losses, with descriptive PPL close to its own dense reference.
The actual-logit upper control demonstrates retained-mass headroom while
confirming that neither local output error nor token NLL follows monotonically
from mass capture.
The fixed encoder gate further identifies coarse-ball collapse associated
with pooled PCA Q/K mean separation, while all tested aggregate value errors
remain above recency's. More occupied balls therefore do not establish a useful
language-model component.
The learned bridge improves binary hard-selection model loss over each initial
state on separate development articles. Ternary combines small descriptive
loss changes with lower logical unions than the concrete feature-matched flat
controls, while locality controls still require fewer positions and less state.
The subsequent group-shared comparison limits every sparse union to 128
positions. Ternary improves over binary at 2,048 tokens in every seed, but
an ordinary coarsened binary hierarchy improves over both, and uniform
coverage has smaller model loss still. Identical-partition grids are worse
than their respective prime hierarchies in all long-context seeds, showing
a comparison-specific hierarchy effect rather than a uniquely \(p\)-adic
arithmetic advantage. The untouched test then supports limited first-layer
quality non-inferiority under the frozen allowance for both finite families,
including stronger ordinary comparators and affected-target losses. That
does not settle a competitive quality--resource operating point or establish
superiority over those controls. Broader supported workloads and mechanistic
attribution remain open; the secondary component-cost study is complete.
The observed \(p=2,3\) difference is specific to these encoders, precisions,
tie rules and workload; it does not select a universal prime.
The native single-token address-identity gate fails at exact quantile
cutpoints despite passing all selected-set attention arithmetic checks.
The distinct native PG-19 quality study now supplies a scoped negative
transfer result. The completed component-cost study characterizes its specified operator.
A smaller ternary point loss is not joint non-inferiority, a unique
\(p\)-adic quality benefit or a quality--resource advantage. The conceptual
conclusion is limited feasibility with unresolved transfer and attribution;
latency alone cannot decide the broader mathematical hypothesis.

\subsection{Teacher, encoder, supervision and hard fitting}

\section{Semantic prefixes, exact contracts and unseen-intent generalization}
\label{app:semantic-structure}

actual text-classification task. It tests a finite \(2\)-adic label geometry
with real LLM features and real conditional probabilities; no neural
arithmetic is replaced by \(\Qp\). Previous quality, FFN and cost outcomes
remain unchanged. Hierarchical probability decomposition is established
prior work \citep{morin2005hierarchical}, and the equivalent ordinary tree
is an exact control rather than a different quality model.

\subsection{Four structural properties and their precise limits}

For integral states, let \(\pi_k(x)=x\bmod p^k\). Reduction from
\(\Z/(p^K\Z)\) to \(\Z/(p^k\Z)\), \(k\leq K\), satisfies
\[
 \pi_k(x+y)=\pi_k(\pi_k(x)+\pi_k(y)),\qquad
 \pi_k(xy)=\pi_k(\pi_k(x)\pi_k(y)).
\]
Differences between representatives are multiples of \(p^k\), so both
identities follow by substitution. This is a ring-homomorphism contract,
not a real approximation guarantee or a claim about arbitrary division.
We do not assign semantic meaning to adding two taxonomy-label addresses.

If \(F=f\circ\pi_k\), then \(F(x+p^k u)=F(x)\) for integral \(u\).
A fixed-depth label decision consequently ignores later digits exactly.
For an LLM interface \(E(h)\), however, the conclusion requires
\(\pi_k(E(h'))=\pi_k(E(h))\); a small real change in \(h\) need not satisfy
this premise. No robustness to embedding noise, paraphrases or adversarial
inputs is inferred from this algebra alone.

For a real probability distribution \(\mu_K(\cdot\mid h)\) on finite
addresses, define
\[
 \mu_k(r\mid h)=\sum_{a:\,a\equiv r\pmod{p^k}}\mu_K(a\mid h).
\]
Partitioning the sum proves consistency under repeated reduction. In a
conditional prefix model,
\[
 \log\mu_K(c\mid h)=\sum_j\log q_j(a_j\mid a_0,\ldots,a_{j-1},h),
\]
and summing descendants yields the corresponding prefix probability.
Unoccupied branches have zero mass; unary branches have probability one.
A flat classifier can obtain the same aggregation contract. Consistent
probabilities do not guarantee consistent maximum-probability decisions:
two leaves in category A with masses 0.3 each and one in B with mass 0.4
have fine maximum B but coarse maximum A. A rule enforcing a common path
would change the decision policy and needs separate quality evaluation.

For the aligned semantic tree and fixed real features, the joint training
objective has a further useful structural property. Each head's binary
cross-entropy appears only for samples passing through that node, with
weight two above the domain level and one below it. Hence
\[
 \mathcal L=\frac1N\sum_i\sum_{v\in\operatorname{path}(c_i)}
 w_v\,\operatorname{BCE}(a_{iv},\sigma(W_vh_i+b_v))
 +\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_v\|W_v\|^2,\qquad w_v\in\{1,2\}.
\]
Because binary cross-entropy is convex in an affine logit, this objective
is convex and separable across heads when the encoder is fixed. Six
initial/final checks reproduce the implemented joint loss within
\(10^{-10}\). The same guarantee holds for the equivalent ordinary tree;
it neither ensures good test accuracy nor extends to jointly training
an LLM encoder. Shuffling semantic domains across branches changes this
aligned decomposition, so its empirical comparison combines inductive bias
and optimization rather than isolating one mathematical field.

These identities also provide checkable explanations of the classifier's
computation. Recorded conditional branch scores reproduce a domain's
probability, but do not explain the causal origin of LLM features or establish
human-readable meaning for every intermediate branch. Four mathematical
checks include 23,460 small quotient/codec identities for primes 2, 3, 5,
independent scalar tree traversal and real-objective finite differences.
They passed locally and on Spark before model execution. Standard algebra
and prefix equivalence are not new theorems.

\section{Continuous disk learning and a modular next-answer interface}
\label{si:continuous-learning}

\subsection{A continuous path without replacing the field by real floats}

Ordinary \(\Qp\) is totally disconnected. A real-valued point loss can be
locally constant under small \(p\)-adic changes, so substituting the usual
real gradient is unjustified. Salazar et al. instead optimize in a
path-connected hull of disks \(\zeta_{c,r}\), with \(c\in\Qp\) and real
radius \(r\geq0\) \citep{salazar2026continuous}. Equivalent representatives
satisfy \(|c-c'|_p\leq r\). At \(r=0\) one recovers an ordinary point;
a positive radius supplies a path along which a real loss can change.
The center remains \(p\)-adic: for example \(-1/9\) has
\(|-1/9|_3=9\), despite its small ordinary magnitude. This is a new learning
interface for our study, not a new low-bit float format or a replacement
for real language-model likelihood.

For a scalar affine output and exact integer inputs \(x_j\),
\begin{equation}
 C=\sum_jx_jc_j,\qquad R=\max_j|x_j|_p r_j,
 \qquad S=\max\{|C|_p,R\}.
\end{equation}
An independent bias is the coordinate \(x_j=1\). Direct regression uses
\(\max\{|C-y|_p,R\}-R/2\); a class output uses real score
\(-\ln S\). Centers in \(\Z[1/p]\), including negative and fractional
values, are represented exactly here by an integer numerator over \(p^2\).
Positive radii and softmax remain FP64 real quantities.

At a disk vertex \(r=p^{-n}\), descending via digit \(\rho\) chooses
\(c'=c_0+\rho p^n\) and decreases the radius; \(c_0\) retains center
digits below exponent \(n\). Ascending increases the radius. Steps stop
at the next vertex, and descending stops at the specified precision cap.
For simultaneous radius directions \(\dot r_j\),
\begin{equation}
 \dot R=\max_{j:\,|x_j|_pr_j=R}|x_j|_p\dot r_j.
\end{equation}
The maximum of tied rates cannot be replaced by their sum. Holding one tied
coordinate fixed can block another's downward motion. Our small affine
implementation therefore enumerates joint incident directions within one
output group, computes analytic one-sided slopes, chooses a seeded uniform
minimum-slope tie, and backtracks against training loss. This differs from
the paper's approximate coordinate queue; enumeration is combinatorial and
establishes no practical large-model scaling or convergence guarantee.

\subsection{Independently audited small learning check}

The public repository inspected on 4 October 2026 contained arithmetic
primitives but no released training optimizer. We implemented the mechanism
independently, without copying upstream training code. Five mathematical
fixtures check signed/fractional centers, representative invariance, joint
radius derivatives, ordinary quotient equivalence and classification slopes.
The illustrated \(p=3\) path starts at center 14, radius \(2/27\), target 23,
rate \(8/81\); twenty scheduled updates reach center 23 and radius \(3^{-6}\).

A separate constructed regression draws three target coefficients in
\(0,\ldots,242\) and two integer covariates in \(0,\ldots,80\), with
constant 1. Each of training, validation and test has 512 independent draws;
targets are \(x\cdot\theta+243\eta\), with independent
\(\eta\in\{0,\ldots,26\}\). All coefficients start at center 0/radius 1;
target coefficients are excluded from fitting. Seeds 17/29/43 each accept
five joint updates and recover all coefficients modulo 243. Every held-out
point error is at most \(1/243\); final direct loss is
0.00205761316872428. The fixed schedule is 100 updates, rate 2 and minimum
radius \(3^{-5}\), with no tuning.

The independent Fraction auditor regenerates the draws and verifies 155,157
trajectory loss elements and every endpoint, with \(10^{-14}\) tolerance
for floating conversion. The frozen regression inputs, scientific code
and compact verified endpoints accompany the paper.
This is mechanism reproduction on a new simple regression, rather than
replication of the cited paper's full tables or a comparative optimization
advantage.

\subsection{Separately frozen typed-operand answer adaptation}

After the audited mechanism gate, a new protocol freezes addition modulo 4
and 9. The original SmolLM2-360M checkpoint provides next-token answer logits;
a learned external branch receives the typed operands \((a,b,1)\) and adds
its class scores to those logits. Every method selects one digit from
\(0,\ldots,m-1\) under identical constrained decoding, then terminates.
No learned parser, computed sum or target is supplied at inference. This is
an answer component with genuine LLM priors, not end-to-end \(p\)-adic
Transformer training or unrestricted text generation.

For each task, model primes 2 and 3, including the deliberate mismatch,
use three trainable coefficient disks per class, initially center 0/radius
\(p^2\). All three fixed seeds 17/29/43 perform 300 full sweeps in randomized
class order, rate \(100p^2(1-1/p)\), depth 6, temperature 1 and fixed branch
weight 1. No separating initialization, point extraction, validation tuning
or post hoc calibration is used. The actual objective is conditional digit
cross-entropy of LLM prior plus \(-\ln S_k\).

For every ordered operand residue pair, there are 8 training and 4 each
validation, same-range test and larger-number test examples. The first three
use operands 0--999, with no pair overlap; larger operands 100000--999999
are outside that range. Alternative wording reuses precisely the larger
pairs, so these transfer tests are correlated and never pooled as independent
observations. Per-task counts are 128/64/64/64/64 and
648/324/324/324/324, respectively. Generator seed \(20261004+m\), literal
prompts and every split rule are fixed before outputs.

Ordinary real controls add either standardized raw affine features or four
Fourier operand products plus a constant. The Fourier products are
\(\cos a\cos b,\cos a\sin b,\sin a\cos b,\sin a\sin b\), with angles
\(2\pi(a\bmod m)/m\) and \(2\pi(b\bmod m)/m\); they do not receive the sum.
Both train 300 full-data Adam steps at 0.03, from zero, with no tuning.
These deterministic controls fit once per task. Native disks store
\(3m\) int64 centers and \(3m\) FP64 radii (\(48m\) numeric bytes), versus
\(5m\) Fourier parameters (\(40m\) bytes) and \(3m\) raw parameters
(\(24m\) bytes), excluding shared LLM/prior state. Rates and derivative
work differ; optimization and representation are not causally isolated.

An ordinary quotient-tree evaluator independently computes congruence depths
from identical learned states. It matches every native logit on all splits
to \(10^{-12}\). This is an exact hypothesis equivalence, not an additional
independent fit, and precludes a uniquely \(p\)-adic advantage claim against
ordinary congruence arithmetic.

\subsection{Post hoc exact learned-rule certificate}

After the frozen fusion gate fails, subtracting only the stored frozen LLM
prior reveals 100\% standalone branch accuracy on every recorded split in
all matched seeds. No parameters, radius, temperature or data are refitted.
An independent Fraction/divisibility check verifies 57,096 standalone
logit elements and the following elementary certificate.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}[Learned congruence rule; post hoc certificate]
Let \(m=p^2\) and class \(k\) have center
\((A_ka+B_kb+C_k)/m\), with integer numerators satisfying
\begin{equation}
 A_k\equiv B_k\equiv u_k\pmod m,\quad
 C_k\equiv-u_kk\pmod m,\quad p\nmid u_k,
 \qquad r_{k,j}\leq1.
\end{equation}
For integer operands, the standalone correct-class logit exceeds every
incorrect-class logit by at least \(\ln p\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the correct residue \(k=(a+b)\bmod m\), the numerator is divisible
by \(m\), hence the center norm is at most 1. Integer input norms and all
coefficient radii are at most 1, so the output radius and seminorm are
at most 1. For any incorrect class \(l\), the numerator is congruent to
\(u_l(k-l)\), nonzero modulo \(p^2\). Its valuation is at most 1;
after division by \(p^2\) its norm is at least \(p\). Applying \(-\ln S\)
gives the stated margin.
\end{proof}
\end{samepage}

All six fitted matched-prime heads satisfy these exact coefficient conditions.
Thus they learn the modular decision rule, rather than merely a finite lookup.
The certificate concerns every integer pair in the mathematical affine model;
the int64 implementation retains its stated bounded-input limits. It is an
ordinary congruence identity, not a new theorem, unlimited-precision software,
or a language-input robustness claim. In particular, typed operands bypass
parsing. The independently implemented ordinary quotient tree satisfies the
same certificate, and real Fourier features also learn the periodic structure.

The fixed LLM prior can outweigh the guaranteed modest branch margin.
Larger-number and new-wording examples have identical operands and branch
scores, yet different fused decisions: that difference comes from the LLM
prior. The failed primary fusion gate is retained alongside this positive
learned-rule diagnostic. No temperature/weight search or subsequent expansion
is conducted. Prime 2 matches \(4=2^2\) and prime 3 matches \(9=3^2\);
this task dependence does not establish a universally best radix.

\subsection{Independent numerical checks and reproducibility}

The independent audit verifies all 150 endpoint records, 114,192 native
logit elements, 4,677 accepted updates and their monotone losses, four
complete 300-step real-fit replays using separate Torch autograd/Adam,
all input tokenizations and ten fresh 32-row LLM fixtures that reproduce

separate full-vocabulary diagnostics. Unrestricted immediate one-token

Fourier is 0\% except 0.31\% for mod 9/new-wording. These diagnostics are
not multistep generation and do not measure a continuation after a leading
space. They make the constrained endpoint's limits explicit.

Stable log-sum-exp evaluates the finite classification losses directly
from logits and avoids probability-space underflow. The compact published
materials include prompts, typed operands, target residues, frozen LLM priors,
all endpoint summaries and the post hoc learned-rule certificate. The
scientific protocol and exact input versions accompany the code; bulk fitted
trajectories and model outputs are regenerated as needed.
